# The Critical Scholar: AI as a Capability-Forming Academic Method

**Date:** 8 September 2026  
**Status:** exploratory programme concept + candidate empirical research programme  
**Publication sensitivity:** open  
**Protocol:** Agalmic Research Memory Palace Protocol v0.3 + Research Provenance Protocol v0.1  
**Parent branch:** Human epistemic capacity through AI engagement

## Triggering artefact

The human curator supplied the full programme concept **The Critical Scholar: A programme for responsible, rigorous and intellectually developmental use of large language models in MPhil study**.

The programme is explicitly framed not as generic AI literacy, but as an advanced academic method for using LLMs to expose weak reasoning, accelerate scholarship and deliberately increase the scholar's own intellectual capabilities rather than outsource them.

Its governing proposition is:

> The purpose of AI-assisted scholarship is not to remove intellectual effort, but to redirect effort toward the forms of reasoning, judgement, synthesis and originality that matter most.

Its one-sentence institutional proposition is:

> Teach students to use AI not to think for them, but to make it increasingly difficult for them to think poorly.

## Core programme architecture

The programme develops five functions:

1. **Knowledge interrogation** — use the model to expose conceptual commitments, alternatives, boundary cases and difficult-to-explain observations rather than merely retrieve explanations.
2. **Position interrogation** — force the student to state, defend and revise their own position, including what evidence would change it.
3. **Intellectual self-assessment** — use oral questioning, escalating difficulty, transfer tasks and explanation at multiple levels to expose the boundary of the student's understanding.
4. **Research augmentation** — use AI for orientation, search-space construction, hypothesis generation, method comparison and adversarial review while preserving the rule that generated claims are not evidence.
5. **Responsible academic production** — use AI for critique, structure and expression while preserving authorship, accountability, evidential traceability and the student's capacity to defend substantive claims.

The eight-session structure proceeds from model limitations and epistemic risk through Socratic examination, concept interrogation, position mapping, adversarial review, literature discovery, writing, and finally metacognitive audit of what should be automated, augmented or deliberately retained as unaided human practice.

The optional **Intellectual Gymnasium** operationalizes deliberate practice through short drills such as explain, compress, expand, transfer, invert, falsify, defend, connect, distinguish and teach.

The **CLAIM** protocol provides a compact evaluation loop:

- **C — Clarify:** What exactly is being claimed?
- **L — Locate:** Where does the evidence come from?
- **A — Attack:** What is the strongest counterargument?
- **I — Inspect:** What assumptions, uncertainties or alternatives remain?
- **M — Make your own judgement:** What do you conclude?

The final step is intentionally non-delegable.

## The central intellectual distinction

The supplied programme makes an important distinction sharper than the earlier local wording of substitution versus augmentation.

There are at least three modes of AI interaction:

### 1. Capability substitution

The model performs cognitive work the human would otherwise perform.

Examples: generating an argument, producing a literature summary, answering a conceptual question.

This may improve immediate output while leaving human capability unchanged or weaker.

### 2. Capability augmentation

The model helps the human perform a task better while the tool is present.

Examples: highlighting unsupported claims, presenting rival interpretations, suggesting robustness tests.

This can increase joint system performance without proving durable human learning.

### 3. Capability formation

The interaction is deliberately structured so the human repeatedly practices a transferable intellectual operation: discrimination, calibration, reconstruction, counterargument, conceptual precision, error detection, methodological defence or similar skills.

The strongest evidence of capability formation is **improved unaided performance after the scaffold is removed**.

This third category is the most important contribution of the programme as an Agalmic capability-building instrument.

The terminology is descriptive and not asserted as novel.

## Transfer as the critical test

The programme already contains an unusually strong assessment principle:

> Did the technology increase the student's intellectual capability when the technology was removed?

This should become the research programme's principal evidential criterion.

Formally, let:

- `P0` = unaided performance before structured AI training;
- `PAI` = performance with AI assistance;
- `P1` = unaided performance immediately after training;
- `P2` = delayed unaided performance on novel material.

AI assistance demonstrates **system augmentation** when `PAI > P0`.

Evidence of **human capability formation** requires at minimum `P1 > P0`, and stronger evidence requires `P2 > P0` on transfer material not rehearsed with the model.

A programme can therefore produce impressive AI-assisted work while failing its central educational objective if `PAI` rises but `P1` and `P2` do not.

This is a simple operational distinction, not a claim of new learning theory.

## Relationship to the Authorial Capacity Constraint

The Authorial Capacity Constraint identifies the risk that machine-assisted artefacts outrun the human curator's ability to understand or defend them.

The Critical Scholar programme turns that constraint into a training target.

It therefore adds a third response alongside provenance and handoff:

1. **Provenance:** preserve what the model contributed and what supports the claim.
2. **Handoff:** route work to missing expertise when the current curator cannot responsibly defend it.
3. **Capability formation:** where feasible, use the interaction to increase the curator's future capacity to understand, evaluate and defend related claims.

This is important because handoff and training solve different problems. Some epistemic deficits should be routed to specialists; others are appropriate targets for deliberate learning.

## Relationship to epistemic artefact filtering

The programme can be adapted directly to the Epistemic Artefact Intake Room.

Instead of merely assigning an artefact a warrant or commitment state, promotion review can become an intellectual drill. For each candidate artefact the human curator could be asked to:

- restate the claim without model language;
- classify it as empirical, conceptual, normative, formal or mixed;
- identify the strongest evidence actually available;
- distinguish source-backed claims from model inference;
- identify assumptions and hidden dependencies;
- generate the strongest counterargument;
- specify a reversal condition;
- identify missing expertise;
- assign and justify confidence before seeing the model's assessment;
- compare their assessment with the model's;
- explain any update;
- reconstruct the reasoning later without assistance;
- apply the discriminating principle to a different artefact.

The Memory Palace would then preserve not only the artefact and decision, but evidence of the curator's changing evaluative capability.

## Consequence for scarcity-transition thinking

Earlier Agalmic discussion often treated human attention, judgement, verification and expertise as successor scarcities after machine cognition makes candidate generation cheap.

The Critical Scholar framework demonstrates why that simplification is unsafe.

Some human complements are **endogenous capacities** whose effective supply can change through:

- deliberate practice;
- education;
- scaffolded AI interaction;
- better representations;
- improved metacognitive calibration;
- specialization and handoff;
- automation of lower-value evaluative work.

The relevant dynamic is therefore not simply:

`cheap generation -> scarce judgement`

but potentially:

`cheap generation -> greater evaluation burden -> investment in evaluator capability -> increased evaluative capacity -> successor bottleneck changes again`

This connects directly to established human-capital accumulation, learning-by-doing and induced-innovation mechanisms. No novelty is claimed for endogenous skill formation itself.

## Why this is more than an AI-literacy course

The programme's strongest institutional frame is **AI as Academic Method**.

That distinction is substantive rather than cosmetic if the programme is assessed by intellectual transfer rather than tool proficiency.

An ordinary AI-skills programme might ask whether students can use a model safely and effectively.

The Critical Scholar asks whether sustained interaction changes the scholar's capacities for:

- conceptual discrimination;
- argument construction;
- evidence appraisal;
- confidence calibration;
- methodological reasoning;
- intellectual independence;
- oral defence;
- recognition of uncertainty;
- transfer across unfamiliar cases.

The model becomes part tutor, part adversary, part examiner and part reflective instrument, but the student remains responsible for judgement.

## Adversarial risks

The programme should not be promoted without confronting several failure modes.

### False examiner problem

An LLM acting as a demanding supervisor can itself be wrong, reward fashionable formulations, miss domain-specific subtleties or fabricate criticism. A student may become better at satisfying the model rather than better at scholarship.

**Mitigation:** source-grounded exercises, faculty-designed rubrics, expert sampling, and explicit tasks in which students must reject model criticism.

### Fluency masquerading as metacognition

Students may learn to produce sophisticated-sounding critiques without improving underlying domain knowledge or error detection.

**Mitigation:** unaided transfer, delayed testing, unfamiliar cases, oral defence and domain-expert scoring.

### Adversarialism without judgement

Repeatedly attacking every claim can produce performative scepticism rather than calibrated evaluation.

**Mitigation:** score false rejection as well as false acceptance; require students to identify when evidence is sufficient and when criticism is weak.

### Scaffold dependence

Students may perform well only while the AI supplies questions and counterarguments.

**Mitigation:** progressive fading of scaffolds and delayed unaided assessment.

### Homogenization

If every student uses similar model-generated objections and conceptual maps, scholarship may converge toward the model's learned defaults.

**Mitigation:** compare multiple theoretical traditions, require primary-source engagement, and include exercises that identify what the model systematically overlooks.

### Domain variance

The same protocol will not transfer unchanged across philosophy, qualitative social science, experimental science, mathematics or clinical research.

**Mitigation:** preserve a common epistemic spine but make disciplinary implementations local.

### Integrity and confidentiality

Research data, unpublished results, personal information and sensitive material can make apparently educational AI use inappropriate.

**Mitigation:** institutional tool policies, disclosure rules, data-classification guidance and explicit no-upload categories.

## Candidate empirical programme

The educational claim should be tested against a null:

> **H0: Structured adversarial/metacognitive LLM use improves assisted task performance but does not produce greater durable unaided scholarly capability than ordinary postgraduate instruction or unrestricted AI use.**

A minimal study could randomize or quasi-randomize students into:

1. conventional postgraduate study support without generative AI;
2. unrestricted or ordinary AI use;
3. AI with responsible-use guidance and citations;
4. The Critical Scholar protocol with Socratic interrogation, adversarial review, CLAIM, Intellectual Gymnasium drills and scaffold fading.

### Pre-specified primary outcomes

- unaided error detection;
- claim/evidence discrimination;
- argument quality;
- conceptual transfer;
- confidence calibration;
- ability to identify missing knowledge and appropriate handoff;
- oral defence;
- delayed retention and transfer.

### Secondary outcomes

- AI-assisted task quality;
- research-question quality;
- time cost;
- student confidence;
- false rejection of valid claims;
- over-reliance;
- disciplinary differences;
- student attitudes toward AI.

The key contrast is between **assisted performance** and **post-assistance capability**.

## Programme design principle

The programme suggests a general design rule for educational AI:

> **Automate work whose performance is the goal. Preserve or deliberately intensify work whose practice is itself how the learner acquires an important capability.**

This is provisional synthesis, not a novel pedagogical law.

It provides a principled way to answer the delegation question. The same intellectual operation may appropriately be automated for an expert under time pressure and deliberately retained for a learner whose objective is to acquire that competence.

## Epistemic status

- AI literacy includes critical and metacognitive capacities: **established antecedent**.
- Structured interventions can alter AI reliance and critical engagement: **supported antecedent family**.
- Better AI-assisted performance does not necessarily imply better human metacognition: **supported warning**.
- The Critical Scholar programme is a coherent educational design: **curator-generated programme artefact, conceptually developed**.
- The distinction between assisted performance and durable unaided transfer is methodologically strong: **established learning-science principle applied here**.
- The Critical Scholar programme measurably increases MPhil scholarly capacity: **untested empirical claim**.
- The programme constitutes a distinct educational contribution beyond existing AI literacy / metacognitive AI pedagogy: **novelty unassessed; requires dedicated lineage review**.

## Decision record

### `decision:promote-critical-scholar-to-testable-programme-artefact`

- **Decision:** preserve The Critical Scholar as a substantive programme artefact and candidate empirical research programme.
- **Reason:** the framework contains a coherent curriculum, assessment architecture and explicit transfer criterion rather than only a set of prompting tips.
- **Commitment:** option / high-value candidate, not automatically Active Frontier.
- **Reversal condition:** narrow it to ordinary AI-literacy pedagogy if dedicated lineage review shows no distinct programme contribution and empirical testing finds no durable transfer benefit.

### `decision:use-transfer-as-primary-capability-criterion`

- **Decision:** distinguish system augmentation from human capability formation using performance after scaffold removal and delayed transfer.
- **Reason:** assisted output quality is insufficient evidence of human intellectual development.

### `decision:treat-human-successor-scarcity-as-endogenous`

- **Decision:** do not model human judgement or verification capacity as fixed in Agalmic scarcity-transition analysis.
- **Reason:** education, deliberate practice and AI-mediated training can alter effective supply.

## Roads not taken

### Rejected

- Classifying the programme primarily as prompt engineering.
- Treating polished AI-assisted coursework as evidence of learning.
- Assuming every model criticism deserves acceptance.
- Using self-reported confidence as the principal measure of improvement.

### Deferred

- Dedicated systematic literature review of postgraduate AI pedagogy and metacognitive training.
- Full curriculum materials and facilitator handbook.
- Ethics submission and trial protocol.
- Psychometric instrument development.
- Public website promotion.

### Surfaced but unexamined

- Adaptive Intellectual Gymnasium difficulty based on demonstrated weaknesses.
- Automatic generation of training exercises from the student's own Memory Palace / epistemic artefact corpus.
- Spaced retrieval and scaffold fading schedules.
- A longitudinal individual epistemic-capability profile.
- Integration with Nemosyne for visualization of conceptual and evaluative growth.
- Whether a similar method can train expert researchers, not only students.
