## Material distinction

The contribution exposes two different ways of responding to epistemic abundance:

1. **External filtering capacity** — improve rankings, provenance, triage, agents, Constitutional Attention, expert routing, or other machinery that selects among epistemic artefacts.
2. **Human evaluator capacity** — improve the person's ability to interrogate, compare, calibrate, verify, reject, assimilate, and defend epistemic artefacts.

These are complementary rather than competing strategies.

The second route is especially important because earlier Agalmic work often treated attention, judgment, verification and expertise as successor scarcities after machine cognition makes candidate generation cheap. The curator's observation implies that at least part of this successor scarcity is **endogenous and trainable**.

A human-AI system may therefore exhibit a feedback loop such as:

`AI lowers candidate-generation cost -> evaluation burden rises -> structured AI engagement trains metacognitive/evaluative capacity -> effective evaluation capacity rises -> new bottlenecks appear`

This does not abolish human limits. It changes the assumption that the human-side bottleneck is fixed.

## Lineage search and novelty discipline

The broad idea has strong antecedents and should not be named as a novel Agalmic construct at this stage.

### Epistemic vigilance

Sperber et al. (2010) describe cognitive mechanisms by which humans evaluate communicated information and guard against misinformation. This is a direct mature antecedent for treating evaluation of communicated claims as a human capacity rather than merely an external filtering problem.

### AI literacy and metacognitive AI literacy

Recent AI-literacy research explicitly moves beyond operational skill toward critical and metacognitive engagement with AI.

Shapiro, Souto-Otero and Watermeyer (2026) define **metacognitive AI literacy** around recognition of epistemic uncertainty, monitoring and regulation of reliance on AI output, and reflection on how human-AI interaction shapes judgment and agency.

Sidra and Mason (2025/2026) validate separate measures of **Collaborative AI Literacy** and **Collaborative AI Metacognition**, finding that collaborative-AI metacognition explains variance beyond general metacognition.

The AI Literacy Heptagon literature likewise includes critical-thinking and integrational dimensions rather than reducing literacy to prompt mechanics.

### Performance can outpace metacognition

Fernandes et al. (2026) report a disconnect between improved reasoning performance under LLM use and users' ability to assess their own performance accurately. This provides a strong empirical warning: increasing task performance is not equivalent to increasing epistemic capacity.

This result closely connects to the existing Authorial Capacity Constraint. AI may help produce stronger artefacts while leaving the human curator unable to judge, reconstruct, or defend the basis for those artefacts.

### Cognitive forcing and over-reliance

Buçinca, Malaya and Gajos (2021) show that cognitive forcing interventions can reduce over-reliance on AI recommendations, although with usability trade-offs. This supports the possibility that interaction design can deliberately induce more analytical engagement rather than simply present better AI output.

### Self-regulated and reflective learning

Current LLM tutoring research increasingly embeds planning, self-monitoring, reflection and learner-state models. The relevant antecedent is not simply 'Socratic prompting' but the wider literature on self-regulated learning, metacognition, desirable difficulty, reflective practice and learning through explanation.

## Provisional synthesis

The useful local synthesis is:

> **AI engagement can be designed not only to substitute for human cognition, but to exercise and expand the human capacities required to evaluate AI-assisted knowledge.**

This is an **application/synthesis** claim, not a novelty claim.

A helpful design distinction is between:

- **capability-substituting interaction** — the AI performs reasoning, evaluation or production that the human would otherwise have to perform;
- **capability-forming interaction** — the interaction is structured so the human must practice framing, discrimination, calibration, counterargument, verification, reconstruction or other transferable epistemic skills.

The phrase `capability-forming interaction` is used here as local descriptive wording only. It has not undergone an exact terminology prior-art search and should not be presented as coined theory.

## Relationship to the Authorial Capacity Constraint

The Authorial Capacity Constraint asks what happens when machine-assisted output can exceed the curator's capacity to understand or defend it.

This continuation adds a possible response:

> **Do not treat authorial capacity only as a ceiling to respect. Treat some components of it as capabilities that can be deliberately trained.**

The system should therefore distinguish at least three objectives:

1. improve the artefact;
2. preserve provenance and route claims to appropriate authority;
3. increase the curator's own ability to evaluate and defend future artefacts.

A workflow can succeed on (1) while failing on (3).

## Relationship to epistemic artefact filtering

The Epistemic Artefact Intake Room currently separates cheap capture from costly promotion. This continuation suggests that promotion decisions can themselves be used as training episodes.

A structured evaluation interaction could ask the human to perform operations such as:

- state the claim in their own words;
- identify what would falsify or weaken it;
- separate externally sourced evidence from model-generated inference;
- identify assumptions and hidden dependencies;
- compare the strongest alternative explanation;
- predict what an expert critic would attack;
- inspect provenance and source quality;
- assign confidence before seeing the model's assessment;
- update confidence after evidence;
- state what cannot currently be defended;
- identify the appropriate handoff;
- reconstruct the argument without model assistance;
- apply the learned discriminating principle to a new artefact.

The goal is not to maximize friction. It is to spend human effort where it produces **transferable evaluative capability**.

## Testable research programme

The important empirical question is not whether users *feel* more critical after using such a framework.

A defensible pilot should test whether structured AI engagement improves measurable epistemic performance.

### Candidate outcomes

- error detection on AI-generated claims;
- discrimination between supported and unsupported claims;
- source-quality discrimination;
- confidence calibration;
- resistance to persuasive but incorrect model output;
- ability to generate relevant counterarguments;
- ability to identify missing expertise / handoff needs;
- unaided reconstruction of an argument after delay;
- transfer to novel epistemic artefacts;
- retention of domain knowledge;
- time and cognitive burden;
- false rejection of good artefacts as well as false acceptance of bad ones.

### Minimal comparative design

Possible conditions:

1. ordinary unrestricted AI interaction;
2. AI with explanations / citations;
3. structured metacognitive evaluation framework;
4. structured framework followed by delayed unaided evaluation.

The most important outcome is **transfer**. If the framework only makes the user perform better while the scaffolding is present, it is useful decision support but not yet evidence of increased human capacity.

A stronger result would be improved performance on new artefacts without the framework present.

## Consequence for Agalmic scarcity thinking

This branch modifies a simplifying assumption in the current abundance discussion.

A successor bottleneck such as human judgment should not automatically be modeled as fixed. It may respond through:

- education;
- practice;
- AI-mediated tutoring;
- institutional specialization;
- tooling;
- better representations;
- delegation and handoff;
- automation of low-value evaluative work.

Therefore successor scarcity should be represented dynamically, with the supply of complementary human capability potentially changing in response to the abundance shock.

This has strong antecedents in human-capital accumulation, learning-by-doing, induced innovation and skill formation. The Agalmic contribution, if any, would be in applying those ideas coherently to epistemic capacity under AI-mediated candidate abundance, not in claiming that trainable human capital is new.

## Potential practical programme

This branch could become a concrete Agalmic Research capability-building track rather than another abstract theory.

One natural application is an academic programme in which students use LLMs under protocols that require them to:

- interrogate concepts rather than merely request answers;
- expose their own assumptions;
- generate and attack competing arguments;
- distinguish provenance, contribution and authority;
- calibrate confidence;
- defend claims orally and in writing;
- demonstrate transfer without AI assistance.

Such a programme would treat AI not merely as an output accelerator, but as a training environment for higher-order epistemic practice.

- broad idea that AI literacy includes critical/metacognitive capacities: **established antecedent**;
- structured interaction can reduce over-reliance: **empirically supported antecedent**;
- AI can improve performance without improving metacognitive accuracy: **empirically supported warning**;
- the supplied shared framework increases the curator's own capacity: **human self-report / unverified in this record**;
- systematic use of epistemic-artefact filtering as deliberate human-capacity training: **synthesis / application, exploratory**;
- measurable transfer to unaided evaluation: **open empirical question**.

## Decision record

### `decision:separate-filtering-machinery-from-evaluator-capacity`

- **Decision:** represent external filtering and human evaluator development as separate but interacting capacities.
- **Reason:** improving the selector and improving the person who selects have different mechanisms, metrics and failure modes.
- **Reversible:** yes.
- **Reversal condition:** collapse the distinction if empirical work shows no meaningful independent contribution from evaluator training beyond decision-support tooling.

### `decision:do-not-coin-new-field-term`

- **Decision:** use established metacognitive AI literacy / collaborative AI metacognition lineage before proposing new terminology.
- **Reason:** close prior art already exists.

### `decision:test-transfer-not-self-report`

- **Decision:** treat transfer to new or unaided evaluation as the key evidence for actual human-capability growth.
- **Reason:** improved assisted performance can coexist with poor metacognitive calibration.

## Roads not taken

### Rejected

- Treating better AI-assisted performance as sufficient evidence that human epistemic capacity increased.
- Treating 'AI literacy' as only prompt engineering or tool-operation skill.
- Naming a new Agalmic theory before lineage review.

### Deferred

- Exact decomposition of the inaccessible shared framework.
- A validated psychometric scale specific to Agalmic epistemic artefact evaluation.
- Curriculum design and experimental protocol.
- Integration into the public website or canonical Agalmic definition.

### Surfaced but unexamined

- Whether repeated adversarial dialogue improves epistemic vigilance or instead produces generalized distrust.
- Optimal fading of AI scaffolding as human capacity increases.
- Whether Nemosyne could visualize the learner's evolving epistemic-capability profile.
- Adaptive difficulty based on demonstrated evaluation performance.
- Whether the Epistemic Artefact Intake Room can generate spaced evaluation exercises from the user's own research corpus.
