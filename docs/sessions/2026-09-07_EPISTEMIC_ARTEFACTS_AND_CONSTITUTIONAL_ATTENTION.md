## A. Session summary

### Starting question

Increasingly capable cognitive machines make the Agalmic Research problems more acute while also increasing the opportunity for frontier expansion. The initial practical concern was how a human curator can increase the effective use of attention, condition and intuition.

The discussion then changed direction in three important ways.

1. **Attention may be partly sidesteppable rather than a permanent item-level bottleneck.** If a mechanism can search, evaluate and act with bounded harm and externalities, human attention need not inspect every candidate. Human effort can instead move toward objectives, constitutions, constraints, escalation rules, audits and exceptional cases.
2. **Attention is only one human contribution.** Domain mastery, causal understanding, abstraction, tacit knowledge, judgment and other cognitive capacities are also scarce today. Their relative contribution may shrink as machine cognitive capacity grows, so the research question should not assume that any current human bottleneck is permanently human-exclusive.
3. **Machine-assisted search can produce valuable intellectual objects that no particular human yet understands.** Calling such an object merely an "idea" is too weak, but calling it "my knowledge" can overstate the relation between the human and the result. The discussion therefore adopted the established term **epistemic artefact** as a neutral category and separated artefact production, warrant, assimilation and realization.

The human curator then proposed a stronger synthesis: **constitutional attention can be treated as an evolutionary-search architecture**. Human attention is wielded toward defining and improving the conditions of search, including the set of worthwhile futures, harms and externalities, rather than manually evaluating every candidate. The system continuously searches for strategies that displace scarcity and expand the reachable frontier.

### What changed

The programme's earlier shorthand

`ideas -> knowledge -> frontier`

is now considered too coarse for this research direction.

A more useful provisional chain is:

`possibility -> directed search -> epistemic artefact -> warrant -> assimilation -> capability -> reachable frontier`

The chain is intentionally not treated as strictly linear. An artefact may be useful before complete human assimilation, and different agents or institutions may hold different parts of the warrant and understanding.

A second provisional chain describes attention allocation:

`human values / meaningful futures -> constitutional constraints -> autonomous search -> candidate evaluation -> safe action or escalation -> observed consequences -> constitutional update`

This moves the research focus from maximizing direct human review toward maximizing **safe, worthwhile frontier expansion per unit of scarce human intervention**.

### Main conclusions at this checkpoint

- **Epistemic artefact** is useful terminology, but it is not an Agalmic coinage. The term already has a philosophy-of-science lineage.
- **Warrant and understanding should be represented separately.** A result can have strong formal, empirical or reproducibility support while remaining opaque to a particular human.
- **Assimilation is a candidate missing stage in the programme vocabulary.** It denotes the integration of an epistemic artefact into an agent's or community's usable, situated body of understanding and practice. The exact epistemology of this term remains unassessed.
- **Constitutional attention** is currently a local terminology candidate for a narrower synthesis, not a novelty claim. Selective human oversight, escalation and interactive evolutionary computation all provide important antecedents.
- The most interesting delta may be the combination of frontier-search objectives, scarcity displacement, low-attention autonomous exploration, harm/externality constraints, provenance, and escalation into a continuously improving search architecture.
- The programme should not assume that attention, expertise, subject mastery, judgment or any other currently scarce human contribution remains the final bottleneck. The bottleneck itself is a moving research object.

### Unresolved questions

1. What exactly makes an epistemic artefact sufficiently warranted to move from candidate to validated artefact?
2. Is "assimilation" the right established term, or does a more precise literature already exist in epistemology, knowledge management, distributed cognition or philosophy of science?
3. Can knowledge sensibly be attributed to a socio-technical system when no individual human understands the full result?
4. When can a validated but human-opaque artefact responsibly produce capability or action?
5. How should "worthwhile futures" be represented without reducing plural human values to a brittle scalar objective?
6. Can constitutional attention be formalized as constrained multi-objective evolutionary search rather than a single fitness score?
7. How should Goodhart effects, specification gaming, value drift, tail risk and externalities modify the search architecture?
8. When should the system escalate to human or specialist attention, and can those escalation rules themselves evolve safely?
9. Which human contributions remain scarce as machine cognition improves, and which merely appear fundamental because of current technical limits?
10. What empirical system could test whether constitutional attention actually increases frontier movement per unit of human intervention?

### 1. Epistemic artefact

**Current classification:** established antecedent + application/synthesis.

Closest identified antecedent:

- Tarja Knuuttila and Atro Voutilainen, "A Parser as an Epistemic Artifact: A Material View on Models," *Philosophy of Science* 70(5), 2003, 1484-1495. DOI: `10.1086/377424`.

Related knowledge-artefact literature:

- G. Salazar-Torres et al., "Design issues for knowledge artifacts," *Knowledge-Based Systems* 21(8), 2008. DOI: `10.1016/j.knosys.2008.03.058`.
- Carla Simone, "Knowledge artifacts: the implications of incommensurable dimensions for their design," *Data Technologies and Applications* 52(1), 2018. DOI: `10.1108/DTA-03-2017-0013`.

**Agalmic delta, if any:** not the term itself. Candidate contribution is the use of epistemic artefacts as a provenance-bearing intermediate layer between machine-directed search and agent-relative assimilation, capability and frontier expansion.

### 2. Warrant without full human understanding

**Current classification:** established antecedents + synthesis/application.

Closest literature family identified:

- Paul Humphreys' work on epistemic opacity in computational science, including his 2004/2009 formulations.
- Contemporary philosophy of computer simulation and machine learning distinguishes access to outputs, justification and understanding, and treats opacity as agent-relative.

Useful orientation source:

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Computer Simulations in Science," current 2026 revision, section on epistemic opacity: `https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/simulations-science/`.

**Agalmic delta, if any:** treating opacity, warrant, assimilation, provenance and downstream capability as explicit states in a human-machine discovery graph.

### 3. Constitutional attention

**Current classification:** terminology candidate + synthesis; novelty unassessed.

Important antecedent families:

- **Interactive evolutionary computation (IEC):** human subjective evaluation guides evolutionary search when a formal fitness function is unavailable or incomplete. Human evaluation burden is itself a long-recognized problem.
- **Selective / risk-tiered human oversight:** autonomous systems proceed on routine actions and escalate consequential, uncertain or irreversible cases to humans.
- **Meaningful human oversight / meaningful human control:** governance literature examines where human control must remain and how it can fail in complex automated systems.
- **Autonomous scientific discovery:** current systems increasingly automate ideation, experimentation, evaluation and scientific writing, raising the question of how output quality is evaluated at scale.

Examples identified in this preliminary pass:

- Hisao Ishibuchi, Takahiko Sudo and Yusuke Nojima, "Interactive evolutionary computation with minimum fitness evaluation requirement and offline algorithm design," *SpringerPlus* 5, 192 (2016). DOI: `10.1186/s40064-016-1789-1`.
- AWS Well-Architected, Agentic AI Lens, "Human-in-the-loop for critical decisions," 2026: `https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/agentic-ai-lens/agentsec04-bp02.html`.
- ISO/IEC DIS 42105, guidance for human oversight of AI systems, draft stage in 2026.
- Chris Lu et al., "The AI Scientist: Towards Fully Automated Open-Ended Scientific Discovery," arXiv:2408.06292; later end-to-end automation work published in *Nature* in 2026.

**Candidate Agalmic delta:** not merely using humans as a fitness function or escalating risky decisions. The narrower proposal is to allocate scarce human intervention toward defining and revising a **constitutional search landscape** whose objective is worthwhile frontier expansion, whose search operators seek strategies that displace scarcity, whose constraints bound harms and externalities, and whose provenance/evaluation machinery determines what may proceed autonomously and what requires escalation.

No world-novelty claim is made.

## G. Evidence register

1. **Knuuttila & Voutilainen (2022)** supports the conclusion that "epistemic artifact" is established terminology in philosophy of science.
2. **Knowledge-artifact literature (2008, 2018)** weakens any attempt to present artefact-mediated knowledge as a new general concept.

4. **Interactive evolutionary computation literature** supports the analogy between human preference/evaluation and a search fitness signal, while also documenting human-evaluation burden.
5. **Selective human-oversight architectures** support moving human review toward high-risk, uncertain or irreversible cases rather than reviewing every action.
6. **Automated-science systems** support the premise that more of the scientific search/evaluation loop can be delegated to machine systems, although they do not establish that the resulting outputs are reliable knowledge.

### Surfaced but unexamined

- Multi-objective, constrained, lexicographic and satisficing alternatives to scalar evolutionary fitness.
- Mechanisms for constitutional mutation and selection without allowing the optimizing system to rewrite its own safety boundary.
- Whether an epistemic artefact can produce legitimate action without human assimilation.
- Whether socio-technical or collective knowledge is the better framework than an artefact-to-assimilation ladder.
- Whether "assimilation" should be replaced by an established term from absorptive capacity, distributed cognition, epistemology or learning theory.
- How to represent responsibility and legitimacy when epistemic competence migrates from humans to machine systems.

### Excluded by scope

- Patentability analysis.
- A general theory of consciousness or machine personhood.
