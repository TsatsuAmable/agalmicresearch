## Session objective

Adversarially test whether Agalmic Research has a defensible intellectual boundary distinct from mainstream economics, industrial policy, operations/constraint management, innovation and growth economics, and socio-technical transition studies.

The immediate trigger was the human curator's objection that the phrase **"worthwhile futures"** risks importing a planner's vantage point into the field definition. The curator further argued that, if the programme reduces to choosing desirable directions and directing resources toward them, it begins to resemble industrial policy and loses a clear distinction from existing economics.

The candidate repair entering this session was:

> Agalmic Research studies the transformation and displacement of scarcity: how binding constraints become less binding, what new bottlenecks emerge, and whether those bottlenecks can themselves be transformed.

This session was instructed to try to break that distinction rather than defend it.

## Adversarial finding 1: economics cannot be caricatured as allocation under fixed constraints

Lionel Robbins's famous scarcity formulation is historically important, but it is not a sufficient description of modern economics. Modern growth and innovation economics explicitly model technological change, non-rival knowledge, changes in factor scarcity, substitution, and endogenous innovation.

Paul Romer's 1990 endogenous-growth model treats technological knowledge as non-rival and partially excludable and makes technological change arise from intentional investment decisions. This directly defeats any claim that economics is structurally unable to analyse reproducible knowledge or changes in productive possibility.

Daron Acemoglu's 2002 directed-technical-change framework goes further: changes in relative factor supplies and prices affect the direction of innovation, with the elasticity of substitution determining how factor scarcity, technical change and factor prices interact.

**Decision consequence:** retire the contrast "economics allocates scarcity; Agalmics changes constraints." It is false as a description of contemporary economics.

Sources:

- Romer, P. M. (1990), *Endogenous Technological Change*, Journal of Political Economy 98(5), DOI `10.1086/261725`.
- Acemoglu, D. (2002), *Directed Technical Change*, Review of Economic Studies 69(4), DOI `10.1111/1467-937X.00226`.

## Adversarial finding 2: recursive binding-constraint removal is already an established method

The proposed recursive core

`identify binding constraint -> relax it -> observe the new constraint -> repeat`

has extremely close antecedents.

### Growth Diagnostics

Hausmann, Rodrik and Velasco's Growth Diagnostics asks practitioners to identify the binding constraint to growth. Rodrik's later summary is especially damaging to a broad novelty claim: it describes successful practice as sequentially identifying the most binding constraints and removing them with locally appropriate remedies.

This is nearly the same abstract algorithm as the candidate Agalmic formulation, albeit applied to development policy and growth.

### Theory of Constraints

Goldratt's Theory of Constraints is even closer at the systems-management level. Its focusing steps identify a constraint, exploit/subordinate around it, elevate it, and then return to the first step once another constraint becomes limiting.

**Decision consequence:** recursive constraint displacement is an established antecedent family, not the distinctive novelty of Agalmic Research.

Sources:

- Hausmann, R., Rodrik, D., Velasco, A. (2005), *Growth Diagnostics*.
- Rodrik, D. (2010), *Diagnostics before Prescription*, Journal of Economic Perspectives 24(3), 33-44.
- Theory of Constraints Institute, *Five Focusing Steps*.
- Hein, K. (1998), *Continuous Improvement Using Theory of Constraints*, ASQ proceedings.

## Adversarial finding 3: technological progress creating successor bottlenecks is already core innovation economics

General-purpose technology research already studies innovations whose benefits depend on complementary innovation and reorganization.

Bresnahan and Trajtenberg (1995) characterize GPTs by pervasiveness, continuing improvement and innovational complementarities. The implication is that a breakthrough in one layer does not automatically translate into system-level abundance or productivity because complementary capacities must develop.

Bresnahan and Yin (2010) explicitly study **growth bottlenecks**, the reallocation of innovative resources around them, and conditions under which bottlenecks are overcome.

Acemoglu, Autor and Patterson (2023/2024) provide an even closer modern antecedent: uneven innovation across complementary sectors can itself create bottlenecks and depress aggregate productivity gains.

**Decision consequence:** "technology relaxes one constraint and exposes another" is an established mechanism family. The phrase can be retained as an organizing synthesis only.

Sources:

- Bresnahan, T., Trajtenberg, M. (1995), *General Purpose Technologies: Engines of Growth?*, Journal of Econometrics 65(1), 83-108, DOI `10.1016/0304-4076(94)01598-T`.
- Bresnahan, T., Yin, P.-L. (2010), *Reallocating Innovative Resources Around Growth Bottlenecks*, Industrial and Corporate Change 19(5), 1589-1627, DOI `10.1093/icc/dtq048`.
- Acemoglu, D., Autor, D., Patterson, C. (2023), *Bottlenecks: Sectoral Imbalances and the US Productivity Slowdown*, NBER Working Paper 31427; later NBER Macroeconomics Annual.

## Adversarial finding 4: the AI-specific abundance-to-human-bottleneck story is already crowded

The existing Agalmic literature review had already found Simon, Weitzman and recent AI-abundance work. This session found additional close current work.

De Freitas, Israeli, Nave, Timoshenko and Toubia (2026) explicitly propose a **Human Bottleneck Framework** for generative AI and innovation, asking which bottlenecks AI alleviates, deepens, or leaves enduring across ideation, screening/testing, preference measurement, diffusion and market learning.

Wu (2026) explicitly models a capability becoming abundant while expert judgment remains scarce, with induced demand capable of recreating scarcity.

Therefore the sequence

`cheap machine cognition -> scarce human judgment/verification/selection`

cannot support a novelty claim by itself.

Sources:

- De Freitas, J. et al. (2026), *Innovating with Generative AI: A Human Bottleneck Framework*, SSRN 6983818 / arXiv:2608.07504.
- Wu, C. (2026), *When AI Abundance Recreates Scarcity: Expert Judgment, Induced Demand, and Composite Ecosystems*, SSRN 7183798.

## Adversarial finding 5: transition studies already own broad socio-technical regime change

A move from "constraint displacement" to "regime transitions" does not solve the boundary problem by itself.

Socio-technical transition studies, especially the multi-level perspective associated with Frank Geels and related innovation-system work, already study major long-term transformations involving technology, institutions, infrastructure, regulation, user practice, markets and culture. Sustainability-transition research is now an established interdisciplinary field whose explicit object is radical structural change in socio-technical systems.

**Decision consequence:** Agalmic Research should not claim "system transitions involving technology and institutions" as its distinctive object.

Sources:

- Geels, F. W. (2002), *Technological transitions as evolutionary reconfiguration processes: a multi-level perspective and a case-study*, Research Policy 31(8-9), 1257-1274, DOI `10.1016/S0048-7333(02)00062-8`.
- Markard, J., Raven, R., Truffer, B. (2012), *Sustainability transitions: An emerging field of research and its prospects*, Research Policy 41(6), 955-967, DOI `10.1016/j.respol.2012.02.013`.

## Adversarial finding 6: original agalmics supplies a stronger lineage anchor than the current definition

Robert Levin's 1999 formulation defined agalmics as the study and practice of the **production and allocation of non-scarce goods**, motivated especially by software and information that can be copied without materially diminishing the holder's supply.

Levin also explicitly acknowledged that non-scarce goods can require scarce inputs to produce. This is important: the interface between scarce production inputs and non-scarce outputs is present in the original lineage.

Modern economics also studies non-rival goods and knowledge. Romer's work means non-rivality cannot be used to draw a hard disciplinary border around economics.

Nevertheless, Levin's definition identifies a more specific phenomenon than the current "worthwhile futures" formulation: changes in the economic character of goods/capabilities when reproduction or use ceases to be strongly rival or scarce.

**Decision consequence:** preserve the Levin lineage and treat Agalmic Research as an extension/reconstruction of the abundance problem, not as a replacement for economics.

Source:

- Levin, R. (1999), *Agalmics: The Marginalization of Scarcity*.

## Adversarial finding 7: industrial policy is adjacent but not identical

The IMF defines industrial policy as targeted government intervention supporting particular firms, industries or narrowly defined activities in order to achieve national economic or non-economic objectives and steer structural transformation.

That is not necessarily the same activity as Agalmic Research.

Agalmic inquiry can remain actor-neutral and analytically prior to policy. A firm, open-source community, scientist, government, standards body or decentralized market process may all discover or implement scarcity-relaxing transformations. If a government later chooses a particular sector and uses subsidies, procurement, regulation or investment to accelerate one such transformation, that intervention may be industrial policy. The research question is broader than that policy instrument.

**Decision consequence:** the boundary from industrial policy survives only if Agalmic Research does **not** encode a collective social objective into its analytical definition.

Source:

- IMF (2024), *Industrial Policy Coverage in IMF Surveillance: Broad Considerations*.

## The strongest surviving candidate boundary

After the adversarial search, the strongest remaining formulation is not a new law of economics and not a general theory of system transition.

It is a candidate **problem-oriented interdisciplinary research programme** organized around a narrower unit of inquiry:

> **transitions in the economic character of a constraint, especially transitions in which a good, input or capability moves from allocation-dominated scarcity toward highly scalable, low-marginal-cost, low-rivalry or otherwise functionally abundant availability, together with the successor constraints, rents, risks, externalities and access problems created by that transition.**

This formulation is deliberately descriptive rather than teleological.

It does **not** ask a central planner to choose society's preferred future. It asks what changed when a formerly scarce capability became cheap, replicable, elastic or widely accessible; which mechanism caused the change; which scarcity dimensions actually fell; where scarcity moved; which forms of scarcity were merely externalized or institutionally recreated; and what further transformations became technically possible.

### Why this may still justify a separate research programme

The constituent mechanisms are mostly established. The proposed distinction is therefore one of **research object, synthesis and measurement**, not first discovery of the mechanisms.

Agalmic Research would be justified as a distinct programme if it can do work that is not naturally performed by any one antecedent field, for example:

1. compare scarcity-to-abundance transitions across information, computation, energy, production, institutions and cognition using a common operational vocabulary;
2. distinguish technical abundance from effective access, legal/institutional exclusion, congestion, externalized ecological cost and scarce complements;
3. model the succession of constraints across linked layers rather than treating one market, sector or production function in isolation;
4. identify when a productivity improvement is merely incremental versus when it changes the coordination/allocation regime around a capability;
5. derive cross-domain predictions that survive comparison with standard production theory, growth theory, innovation economics, transition studies and operations research.

At present this is **candidate programme distinctiveness**, not demonstrated novelty.

## Working operationalization to test, not yet a new metric

A future formalization should represent scarcity as multidimensional rather than binary. Candidate dimensions include:

- marginal cost of an additional usable unit;
- supply elasticity / scalability over the relevant demand range;
- rivalry and congestion;
- excludability and access friction;
- substitutability;
- complementary-input dependence;
- time/attention required for use or verification;
- ecological or other externalized resource burden;
- concentration or control over the bottleneck.

A transition should count as strongly agalmic only if the relevant scarcity burden falls in the system actually experienced by users, rather than merely moving off the focal balance sheet or into an unmeasured complement.

This list is a working operationalization. No claim is made that the dimensions or combination are novel.

## Field-status verdict

### New economic theory

**No.** Current evidence does not support this description.

### Replacement or alternative to economics

**No.** This framing would be misleading. Economics already contains non-rival knowledge, technological change, constraint relaxation, innovation direction, complementary assets, bottlenecks, induced demand and structural transformation.

### Industrial policy

**No, not inherently.** Particular implementations may become industrial policy when governments target selected activities toward specified national objectives.

### Distinct subfield of economics

**Not established.** The candidate object crosses economics but does not yet have sufficiently distinct theory, measurement or empirical results to warrant that claim.

### Distinct interdisciplinary research programme

**Plausible and currently the strongest defensible status.** Its proposed organizing object is the transition of capabilities from strongly scarcity-bound to functionally abundant regimes and the resulting succession/recreation of scarcity across connected systems.

## Consequence for the canonical definition

The current v0.2 definition should be revised.

The phrase **"worthwhile futures"** should move out of the analytical definition and remain, if retained, as an institutional or ethical selection principle for what Agalmic Research chooses to work on.

A candidate replacement is:

> **Agalmic Research studies transitions in the structure of scarcity: how technological, epistemic, organizational and institutional change can make a previously binding constraint cease to dominate access or system performance; what new constraints, rents, risks and externalities then become decisive; and how such transitions propagate across connected systems. It is especially concerned with transitions toward capabilities that are highly replicable, scalable or inexpensive at the margin.**
>
> **It is not a theory for choosing society's ends, and it is not an alternative to economics. Economics, innovation studies, operations research, engineering and transition studies are among its inputs. Its proposed distinctive object is the transition into and out of functional abundance and the systematic tracing of successor scarcity.**

This is a proposed definition, not yet promoted to canonical status in this session.

## Decision replay

### `decision:remove-worthwhile-futures-from-field-core`

- **Question:** should "worthwhile futures" define Agalmic Research?
- **Decision:** no; recommend moving it to institutional ethics/selection.
- **Reason:** it embeds a normative objective into the field definition and blurs the distinction from mission-oriented/industrial policy.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.
- **Reversible:** yes.
- **Reversal condition:** a rigorous non-planner interpretation of the phrase becomes necessary to distinguish the programme and survives political-economy review.

### `decision:reject-fixed-scarcity-economics-contrast`

- **Decision:** reject.
- **Reason:** endogenous growth, directed technical change, GPT and bottleneck literatures directly contradict the contrast.
- **Confidence:** high.

### `decision:classify-as-interdisciplinary-programme`

- **Decision:** provisionally describe Agalmic Research as an interdisciplinary research programme, not a new economic theory.
- **Reason:** the mechanisms have strong antecedents, while a narrower cross-domain object may still support coherent research.
- **Confidence:** medium.
- **Reversal condition:** retire or fold the programme into an established field if formalization and empirical work produce no distinct explanatory, predictive or measurement value.

## Roads not taken

### Rejected after examination

- "Economics allocates fixed scarcity; Agalmics transforms constraints."
- Recursive constraint displacement as a novel general mechanism.
- Broad socio-technical regime transition as the unique Agalmic object.
- AI generation abundance -> human judgment scarcity as a novel Agalmic mechanism.
- "Worthwhile futures" as the analytical objective function of the field.

### Deferred

- Formal mathematical definition of functional abundance.
- A scarcity-state vector or index.
- Historical cross-domain dataset of abundance transitions.
- Formal null test against standard production/growth theory.
- Revision of the canonical public definition.

### Surfaced but unexamined or incompletely examined

- ecological/resource economics and rebound/Jevons effects;
- complexity economics and network bottleneck propagation;
- cybernetics and control-theoretic constraint models;
- evolutionary economics beyond the selected transition literature;
- economic history of cost collapse and dematerialization;
- artificial/institutionally maintained scarcity beyond selected sources;
- whether "functional abundance" has a mature formal vocabulary that should replace local wording.

### Excluded by scope

- normative theory of which capabilities should be made abundant;
- centralized planning or national priority selection;
- implementation of industrial policy;
- patentability or defensive-publication strategy.
