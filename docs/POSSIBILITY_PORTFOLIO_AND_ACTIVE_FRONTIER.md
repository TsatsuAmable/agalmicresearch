# Possibility Portfolio and Active Frontier

Version: 0.1  
Status: experimental operating method  
Date: 7 September 2026

## Purpose

Agalmic Research operates in a regime where candidate ideas can be generated much faster than a human curator can responsibly evaluate, develop or realize them.

The goal is therefore not to suppress ideation. It is to preserve possibility cheaply while spending scarce attention deliberately.

> **Ideas are abundant inputs. Worthwhile futures are the output.**

This operating method is a pragmatic synthesis of exploration/exploitation, innovation portfolio management, real-options thinking, idea-management research and the adjacent-possible literature. It is not presented as a novel management theory.

## 1. Two spaces

### Possibility Portfolio

The Possibility Portfolio preserves public-safe ideas, questions, conjectures, experiments, paper directions, implementation possibilities and handoff opportunities without implying commitment.

Capture is intentionally cheap.

An idea in the portfolio is an **option**, not a task.

### Active Frontier

The Active Frontier contains the few possibilities currently receiving meaningful scarce resources.

Default work-in-progress limit: **three substantial active bets**.

A fourth item does not enter because it is exciting. It enters only when one active item leaves, capacity demonstrably increases, or the new item clearly dominates an existing commitment.

## 2. Portfolio states

Each possibility has one current state:

- `seed` — captured with minimal interpretation;
- `mapped` — relationship to the programme, prior art and next questions has been clarified;
- `option` — preserved as worthwhile but not currently active;
- `active` — receiving scarce attention on the Active Frontier;
- `handoff` — awaiting or undergoing transfer to missing expertise/capability;
- `realized` — produced a material capability, finding, artefact, decision or validated negative result;
- `parked` — intentionally inactive because timing, capacity, evidence or dependencies are unfavorable;
- `retired` — invalidated, superseded, duplicated by prior art, no longer directionally useful, or otherwise closed.

Retirement does not delete provenance.

## 3. Capture record

A new seed should require only enough information to preserve its option value:

- stable ID;
- title;
- one-paragraph description;
- originating session/source;
- contributor/provenance pointer where available;
- why it seemed interesting at the time;
- related graph nodes/projects;
- publication sensitivity: `public`, `review-before-publication`, `potential-IP-sensitive`, `private` or `unknown`.

If publication sensitivity is not `public`, do not place the substantive idea in the public repository.

## 4. Mapping record

Before promotion beyond `seed`, answer:

### Direction

**What worthwhile future does this make more reachable?**

If that cannot be answered yet, the idea may remain a seed without shame.

### Capacity created

What new capacity could success create?

Examples:

- search capacity;
- representation/recognition capacity;
- validation capacity;
- coordination/handoff capacity;
- realization capacity;
- reusable knowledge;
- reusable software/tooling;
- institutional capability;
- physical capability;
- access or cost reduction;
- option value for future branches.

### Frontier leverage

Does realizing this possibility expose additional valuable possibilities or make later work cheaper/easier?

### Lineage

Is the proposition established, rediscovered, synthesis, extension, application, operationalization, terminology candidate or novelty-unassessed?

If novelty matters to the value proposition, conduct the appropriate lineage search before significant promotion.

### Next discriminating action

What is the cheapest action that would materially change the decision to pursue, park, hand off or retire the idea?

Prefer an experiment, literature search, expert question, prototype, calculation, falsification attempt or user test over more prose.

### Realization path

If the candidate survived the next test, what capability or actor would be needed next?

This may be the curator, software, a domain expert, collaborator, funder, experimental group, standards expert, engineer or institution.

## 5. Frontier Filter

Do not reduce an uncertain idea to a single pseudo-precise score.

For each mapped candidate, record an ordinal judgment with confidence for these dimensions:

- **Worthwhile-future relevance:** weak / moderate / strong;
- **Frontier leverage:** weak / moderate / strong;
- **Information gain from the next action:** weak / moderate / strong;
- **Realization or handoff path:** absent / plausible / clear;
- **Attention burden:** low / medium / high;
- **Lineage confidence:** low / medium / high;
- **Urgency/window:** none / useful-now / perishable;
- **Uncertainty:** low / medium / high.

A candidate normally enters the Active Frontier only if:

1. worthwhile-future relevance is strong;
2. there is a concrete discriminating next action;
3. a frontier slot exists or the candidate clearly dominates an incumbent;
4. obvious lineage work required to justify the next spend has been done;
5. the next commitment is proportionate to uncertainty.

High uncertainty is not itself a reason to reject an idea. It is often a reason to buy information cheaply.

## 6. Capacity-over-output gate

Before promoting or publishing an object, ask:

> **What new capacity does this create?**

A paper, article or public note is directionally useful when it does at least one of the following:

- clarifies a question enough to test;
- maps a literature so later researchers need not repeat the search;
- recruits criticism, expertise or collaboration;
- preserves a reusable model, dataset, tool or protocol;
- creates an empirical or formal test;
- records a negative result that closes a branch;
- enables a handoff;
- makes a valuable possibility easier to recognize or realize.

If publication merely turns one more attractive idea into public text, preserving it in the portfolio may be better.

## 7. Weekly frontier review

Review the portfolio at least weekly while the programme is active.

The review should be short and comparative.

For each Active Frontier item:

1. What changed since the previous review?
2. What did we learn?
3. Did the next action reduce uncertainty or create capacity?
4. Is the item still one of the three best uses of scarce attention?
5. Should it remain active, move to handoff, become an option, park, retire or be marked realized?

Then inspect at least one **challenger** from the option/parked pool:

> Is this challenger now a better use of attention than the weakest active item?

This prevents incumbency from becoming destiny.

## 8. Monthly portfolio review

Once per month, review the shape of the whole portfolio rather than individual ideas.

Check for:

- too much exploration with little realization;
- too much exploitation with no frontier expansion;
- repeated ideas that should be merged;
- ideas kept alive only through emotional attachment;
- ideas that now have obvious prior art;
- missing domains or perspectives;
- bottlenecks shared by several candidates;
- capabilities that, if built once, would unlock multiple branches;
- candidates suitable for epistemic handoff;
- active work that creates artefacts but little future capacity.

## 9. The personal rule

For the human curator:

> **Capture promiscuously. Commit selectively. Realize deliberately.**

Do not stop having ideas.

Stop requiring every idea to become a project.

The purpose of the portfolio is psychological as well as operational: preserving an idea should make it easier to decline immediate commitment without feeling that the possibility has been lost.

## 10. Public versus private preservation

The public Agalmic Research repository can preserve only ideas appropriate for disclosure.

Potentially patentable, confidential, personally sensitive or incompletely assessed material needs a private store using the same schema. The public portfolio should contain only public-safe projections or metadata permitted by the publication/IP decision.

## 11. Success criteria for the pilot

After four weeks of use, review whether this system:

- reduces the number of simultaneous active obligations;
- improves recall of parked ideas;
- increases the fraction of sessions that end with a discriminating next action;
- increases explicit retirement/merging of weak ideas;
- improves lineage checking before publication;
- generates more handoffs when local authority is weak;
- produces more reusable capacity per unit of curator attention;
- remains lightweight enough to use consistently.

If not, simplify it.

A system for managing cognitive abundance that consumes all available cognition has failed.
