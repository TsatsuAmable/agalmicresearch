# Literature Review: Idea Portfolios, Selection and the Active Frontier

Version: 0.1  
Status: working lineage review  
Date: 7 September 2026

## Purpose

Agalmic Research needs a practical way to preserve abundant ideas without turning every idea into an obligation. Before inventing a local system, this note maps mature antecedents in organizational learning, innovation portfolio management, idea management, real options and idea evaluation.

The local design terms **Possibility Portfolio**, **Possibility Garden** and **Active Frontier** are operating metaphors, not claims of conceptual novelty.

## 1. Exploration and exploitation

James G. March's classic 1991 paper distinguishes **exploration of new possibilities** from **exploitation of old certainties** and studies the resource-allocation tension between them. The central lesson for Agalmic Research is that an idea-preservation system must not optimize entirely for either novelty generation or near-term execution.

Source: James G. March (1991), [*Exploration and Exploitation in Organizational Learning*](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2.1.71).

**Implication:** preserve an exploration pool while keeping execution capacity intentionally scarce.

## 2. Innovation portfolio management

Robert Cooper, Scott Edgett and Elko Kleinschmidt study portfolio management as the selection and balancing of development projects under scarce resources. Their work emphasizes strategic alignment, value, balance and avoiding more active projects than available resources can support. Their industry study also cautions against relying only on narrow financial metrics; high-performing firms made substantial use of strategic and scoring approaches.

Sources:

- Cooper, Edgett & Kleinschmidt (2001), [*Portfolio management for new product development*](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9310.00225).
- Cooper, Edgett & Kleinschmidt (1999), [*New Product Portfolio Management: Practices and Performance*](https://doi.org/10.1111/1540-5885.1640333).

**Implication:** the Active Frontier needs an explicit work-in-progress limit and should be judged as a portfolio, not only one idea at a time.

## 3. Ideas as options under uncertainty

Real-options approaches to R&D treat uncertain projects as staged commitments in which managers can defer, expand, accelerate or abandon as information arrives. Lint and Pennings explicitly model product development as a sequence of options whose uncertainty falls over time; Schwartz shows that abandonment flexibility can be a major component of R&D value.

Sources:

- Lint & Pennings (2001), [*An option approach to the new product development process*](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9310.00206).
- Schwartz (2003/2004), [*Patents and R&D as Real Options*](https://doi.org/10.3386/w10114).

**Implication:** an unpursued idea can retain option value. Preservation is not failure. Promotion should buy information in small increments before committing large amounts of scarce attention.

## 4. Idea management is already a field

Idea management has a mature literature covering generation, capture, evaluation, selection and implementation. Jensen's review notes that idea-management systems have long existed across innovation management and software/IT; later reviews provide implementation frameworks for corporate idea-management programmes.

Sources:

- Anna Rose Vagn Jensen (2012), [*A literature review of idea management*](https://www.designsociety.org/publication/38555/a_literature_review_of_idea_management).
- Gerlach & Brem (2017), [*Idea management revisited: A review of the literature and guide for implementation*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijis.2017.10.004).

**Implication:** Agalmic Research should not claim novelty for capturing, filtering or staging ideas. The local value must come from adapting those practices to machine-amplified cognitive abundance, lineage/provenance requirements and a frontier-expansion objective.

## 5. Selection is difficult and biased

Research on creative idea evaluation finds that generation is only part of the innovation problem. Recognition and selection are difficult, and highly original ideas can be disadvantaged because uncertainty and low apparent feasibility make them unattractive. Recent systematic review work synthesizes a large literature on methods, processes, evaluator characteristics, bias and evaluation accuracy.

Sources:

- Rietzschel, Nijstad & Stroebe (2019), [*Why Great Ideas Are Often Overlooked*](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190648077.013.11).
- Baraboshkin, Tuerkyilmaz, Cui & Kirdasinova (2026), [*Unpacking idea evaluation process*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.technovation.2026.103563).

**Implication:** a single score must not become a truth oracle. Evaluation should retain uncertainty, record why an idea was parked or rejected, and deliberately separate originality from immediate feasibility.

## 6. The effective adjacent possible already exists as a construct

The phrase **effective adjacent possible** is not an Agalmic Research invention. Josef Taalbi's work explicitly distinguishes the universe of possible innovations from an effective adjacent possible constrained by search complexity, absorptive/search capacity and available resources. This is directly relevant to the project's meta aim.

Source: Josef Taalbi (2025/2026), [*Long-run patterns in the discovery of the adjacent possible*](https://doi.org/10.1093/icc/dtaf028).

Taalbi's formulation also supports the point that innovation can eventually become constrained by resources available for search even when the combinatorial possibility space continues to grow.

**Implication:** Agalmic Research should cite and build on Taalbi rather than claim the theoretical/effective distinction. The useful extension is to ask how cognitive tools, provenance, validation capacity and realization infrastructure alter the effective adjacent possible for particular human-machine systems.

## 7. Surviving local design problem

The useful problem is therefore not:

> How can we invent a system for storing and ranking ideas?

It is:

> **How should an individual or small research programme operating under machine-amplified cognitive abundance preserve a very large option set while allocating scarce human attention to a very small number of directionally meaningful experiments, handoffs and realization efforts?**

The local system should combine established ideas rather than replace them:

- exploration/exploitation separation;
- portfolio balance and WIP limits;
- staged commitment and abandonment options;
- explicit idea capture and evaluation;
- bias-aware selection;
- intellectual-lineage and provenance tracking;
- a meta objective of expanding the effective adjacent possible rather than maximizing idea count.

## 8. Design consequences

The initial Agalmic Research operating system should therefore:

1. make capture cheap and non-obligatory;
2. separate the preserved option set from active commitments;
3. cap the Active Frontier at three substantial bets unless capacity demonstrably increases;
4. require each active bet to have a small discriminating next action;
5. compare active bets against the best parked challenger during review;
6. record why ideas are promoted, parked, handed off, rejected or retired;
7. retain reversal conditions so rejected branches can reopen when evidence changes;
8. treat lineage search as part of evaluation when novelty matters;
9. assess the **capacity created** by an idea, not only the artefact it might produce;
10. avoid a single total score that conceals uncertainty or evaluator bias.

## Search boundary

This is a targeted literature screen, not a systematic review of the full innovation-management, decision-science, creativity, R&D portfolio, personal knowledge-management or operations-research literatures. The local operating method should therefore be described as a pragmatic synthesis and pilot, not as a validated new framework.
