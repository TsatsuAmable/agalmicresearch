# Literature Review: Displaced Scarcity and Shifting Bottlenecks

Version: 0.1  
Status: deep prior-art review / working economics note  
Date: 7 September 2026

## Question

Does the Agalmic Research **Displaced Scarcity Hypothesis** identify a genuinely new economic mechanism, or is it a synthesis of established ideas about limiting factors, complements, bottlenecks, attention, relative prices and induced innovation?

Working formulation:

> When a previously binding constraint becomes sufficiently abundant, system performance and value become increasingly determined by complementary constraints that remain scarce.

## Bottom line

> **The general intuition is not novel.**

A wide range of established theories already imply that relieving one constraint can expose another, that complementarity makes scarce inputs decisive, that relative prices shift toward difficult-to-expand inputs, and that abundance of information or ideas can produce scarcity in processing and attention.

The current phrase may still be useful as an organizing synthesis, but the project should not imply discovery of a new economic law. A defensible contribution would require a formal theory that states the conditions under which “scarcity displacement” occurs, distinguishes it from ordinary substitution and bottleneck reasoning, and produces empirical predictions.

## 1. Simon is almost a textbook instance

Herbert Simon's 1971 treatment of an information-rich world is the cleanest direct antecedent. His argument is structural: an abundance of information creates a scarcity of what information consumes, namely human attention.

This has exactly the form that motivated the Agalmic hypothesis:

`abundant information → scarce attention`

Simon also frames the resulting problem as resource allocation. The project therefore cannot use information-to-attention displacement as evidence that the general mechanism is newly discovered.

Falkinger later formalises limited attention in an information-rich economy, showing that the intuition has already been taken into equilibrium economics.

## 2. Complementarity and fixed proportions already generate moving constraints

In a Leontief or fixed-proportions production function, output is determined by the input that is scarce relative to the required proportions. Increasing an already excessive input does nothing. Expanding the binding input can cause a different input to become limiting.

This is a mathematically transparent version of “scarcity moves.”

The management literature's Theory of Constraints similarly treats system performance as governed by a current constraint or bottleneck and explicitly expects the relevant constraint to change as improvements are made.

Older limiting-factor ideas in biology and production theory make the same structural point. These antecedents mean that **constraint migration itself is not new**.

## 3. Complementarity can make weak links disproportionately valuable

Kremer's O-Ring theory models production processes in which multiple tasks are strongly complementary and mistakes in any one task reduce the value of the others. Jones (2008) likewise develops models of intermediate-goods linkages, weak links and superstars.

These theories matter because the Displaced Scarcity Hypothesis implicitly assumes complementarity. If the formerly scarce input and the remaining inputs are close substitutes instead, making one abundant may not increase the value of the others in the same way.

### Required correction

The hypothesis must not be stated without asking:

- Are inputs complements or substitutes?
- Is output Leontief-like, Cobb–Douglas, CES or governed by another technology?
- Does abundance reduce the marginal value of the abundant input continuously or cross a binding threshold?
- Does a newly binding complement have elastic or inelastic supply?

Without those conditions, the formulation risks being a verbal restatement of standard production theory.

## 4. Teece shows value migrating toward complementary assets

Teece's 1986 profiting-from-innovation framework is highly relevant to the word **value** in the Agalmic formulation. When imitation is easy, profits from innovation may accrue not to the inventor but to owners of complementary assets required to commercialize the innovation.

That is not identical to a production bottleneck, but it demonstrates that abundance or weak appropriability in one asset can redirect rents toward scarce complements.

Agalmic Economics should therefore distinguish:

- **physical/technical bottleneck:** what limits feasible output;
- **economic scarcity:** what commands a high shadow price or marginal value;
- **rent capture:** who can appropriate value because they control complements or institutions;
- **attention/selection bottleneck:** what limits which candidates receive consideration;
- **realization bottleneck:** what limits conversion of knowledge into practical effects.

Collapsing those into “value migrates” would be too loose.

## 5. Weitzman anticipates idea abundance and processing limits

Weitzman's *Recombinant Growth* is especially important for the Economics of Epistemic Abundance. His model treats new ideas as combinations of existing ideas and argues that the issue may become less an absence of possible recombinant ideas than the ability to process the vast number of combinations into useful innovations.

That strongly anticipates the project's claim that cheap generation can move pressure toward selection, evaluation and development.

The prospective Agalmic contribution must therefore lie beyond “there are too many possible ideas to process.”

## 6. Baumol shows differential productivity changing relative economic weight

Baumol's unbalanced-growth model is another neighboring mechanism. When productivity rises quickly in some sectors but remains difficult to raise in labor-intensive sectors, the stagnant activities become relatively more expensive and absorb greater expenditure shares.

This is not literally a bottleneck model, but it captures a broader comparative-static intuition:

> rapid productivity improvement in one part of a complementary economy can increase the relative cost and salience of sectors where productivity does not keep pace.

That is very close to the economic story Agalmic Research wants to tell about cheap machine cognition and expensive human verification, care, judgment or embodied realization.

## 7. Hicksian induced innovation runs in the reverse causal direction

Hicks's induced-innovation intuition says that changes in relative factor prices can direct technical change toward economizing on the relatively expensive input.

This matters because scarcity displacement may be dynamic rather than permanent. If AI makes generation cheap and raises the relative value of expert judgment, firms have incentives to automate or augment judgment next. Today's complement can become tomorrow's target for abundance.

A serious theory therefore needs feedback:

`constraint becomes scarce → relative price/rent rises → innovation targets constraint → constraint relaxes → another complement may bind`

This would make displaced scarcity an evolving sequence rather than a one-time migration.

## 8. Current AI-era literature is already very close

The novelty space has narrowed further because recent papers explicitly frame AI as abundance in cognitive production that recreates scarcity elsewhere.

Shi's 2026 *Abundance Shock* argues that AI erodes scarcity in cognitive/informational production while judgment and trust remain scarce.

Wu's 2026 SSRN paper *When AI Abundance Recreates Scarcity* is closer still: it models a capability becoming abundant while expert judgment remains scarce, incorporates induced demand, and treats expert attention as divided between current service and feedback that improves future capability.

Kolb and Caron's 2026 preprint *Cheap, Fallible Cognition and the Political Economy of Expertise* argues that when answer generation becomes abundant, scarce human capital shifts toward question selection, framing, interpretation and responsibility.

Sun's 2026 *From Labor to Attention* similarly connects AI abundance with the rising economic importance of finite human attention.

These sources mean Agalmic Research should **not** advertise an AI-specific abundance-to-judgment shift as novel merely because its terminology differs.

## 9. What would make the Displaced Scarcity Hypothesis non-trivial?

A useful formal theory needs to answer questions existing bottleneck/complementarity models do not settle automatically.

### Candidate formal research programme

Let output or social value depend on a vector of complementary capacities:

`Y = F(x1, x2, ..., xn)`

and let technological change sharply reduce the marginal cost or expand the feasible supply of one input `xk`.

The research programme should derive conditions under which:

1. the shadow value of `xk` falls;
2. one or more complements' shadow values rise;
3. expenditure or rents migrate toward those complements;
4. the identity of the binding constraint changes;
5. induced demand can offset the apparent relief in scarcity;
6. innovation incentives target the newly scarce complement;
7. institutional or market power follows the new bottleneck;
8. welfare improves or worsens depending on ownership, externalities and access.

The theory must distinguish at least three cases:

- **strong complements:** abundance exposes a new bottleneck sharply;
- **partial substitutes:** displacement is gradual or ambiguous;
- **induced-demand regime:** cheaper capability expands demand enough that the old or new bottleneck remains congested.

The phrase “displaced scarcity” earns analytical value only if this structure generates predictions beyond saying that scarce things are scarce.

## 10. Empirical predictions worth testing

For AI-assisted knowledge work, plausible predictions include:

- as generation cost falls, a larger share of project time/cost shifts to verification, integration or deployment;
- wages/rents rise for scarce complementary expertise whose supply is inelastic;
- organizations with stronger absorptive/evaluative capacity gain disproportionately from the same AI capability;
- output volume rises faster than validated-output volume;
- queues for expert review lengthen unless triage or verification productivity improves;
- proprietary control of a new bottleneck creates market power even when the generated artefact is non-rival;
- AI adoption induces demand for more candidate work, partially recreating scarcity in expert review;
- innovation subsequently targets the newly expensive complement.

These are testable and could distinguish the project from pure metaphor.

## 11. Current novelty position

The appropriate classification is:

**established antecedents + synthesis/generalization + formal novelty unassessed.**

The term **Displaced Scarcity Hypothesis** can remain as an organizing label, provided every serious paper states that:

- the broad intuition has strong antecedents;
- “law” is not warranted;
- current AI-specific literature contains close formulations;
- the contribution sought is formal conditions, comparative statics, institutional consequences and empirical tests.

## 12. Search boundary

This review searched economics, innovation, management, attention and AI-era abundance literatures through public scholarly sources. It is not exhaustive across EconLit, JSTOR, Scopus, Web of Science, historical production theory, operations research, industrial organization, energy/resource economics or non-English scholarship.
