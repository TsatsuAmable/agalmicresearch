# Agalmic Research: site direction

## Positioning

**Agalmic Research studies what becomes scarce when the generation of ideas, code, models and candidate knowledge becomes cheap.**

The site should feel like a research notebook that has acquired an address, not a startup landing page and not a simulated university institute. Its authority should come from clear claims, visible work, durable citations, open source, version history and the quality of the arguments.

## Core thesis

The working thesis is the **Law of Displaced Scarcity**:

> As one constraint becomes abundant, value migrates toward the complementary constraints required to select, verify, coordinate and realise it.

This is a research hypothesis and organising lens, not a claim that all scarcity disappears.

## Research architecture

1. **Agalmic Economics**: the umbrella programme.
2. **Epistemic Economics**: allocation under abundant candidate knowledge and scarce attention, validation, trust and institutional capacity.
3. **Innovation as Search**: human-model exploration and exploitation of adjacent possibles.
4. **Attention and Selection**: ranking, curation, validation, quality and information-theoretic direction-setting.
5. **Representation as Discovery**: finding lower-cost representations that preserve meaningful information; Nemosyne/Moneta is the applied testbed.
6. **Institutions, IP and Rents**: publication, defensive disclosure, patents and incentives after epistemic abundance.

## Publication model

Treat each project as a canonical, versioned research object. Produce useful projections from it:

- working paper
- peer-reviewed article
- public essay
- policy brief
- talk
- formal model
- code / dataset / demonstration

The website is the index and connective tissue. GitHub is the living source and provenance trail. Stable releases can be deposited in Zenodo, OSF, SSRN or other appropriate archives. Journal publication remains valuable but is not the sole gatekeeper of the work.

## Editorial rules

- State conjecture as conjecture.
- Separate evidence from rhetoric.
- Prefer a precise question over a grand claim.
- Publish early enough to establish provenance and invite criticism.
- Version rather than silently overwrite.
- Do not inflate the organisation. Let the corpus create the institution.
- Use patents exceptionally, where exclusivity is plausibly required to finance or coordinate real-world implementation.
- Do not make AGI a prerequisite. Study the transition already created by inexpensive machine cognition.

## Visual direction

- Text first.
- No hero imagery unless it carries research information.
- Warm paper background, dark ink, one restrained green accent.
- System fonts only.
- Large editorial serif headlines, compact sans-serif metadata.
- Hairline rules and generous whitespace instead of cards, gradients and product-dashboard ornament.
- Zero client JavaScript until there is a research reason for it.

## Information architecture

- `/`: thesis and field framing
- `/research`: research tracks and questions
- `/publications`: paper sequence and publication model
- `/principles`: operating philosophy, open research and IP stance

Add papers/essays as content collections only when the first real publications exist. Add RSS at the same point. Add search only when the corpus is large enough that navigation fails without it.

## Domain strategy

Use **agalmicresearch.org** as the canonical public research address. Redirect secondary domains to it rather than fragmenting the identity. The `.com` can remain valuable defensively and for future commercial tooling, but the research corpus should have one canonical home.
